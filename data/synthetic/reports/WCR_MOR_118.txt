4. DRILLING PROBLEMS & INCIDENT LOG (SECTION 4.2 - CRITICAL HISTORICAL PRECEDENT)
[PAGE 19]
Depth: 2,848.0 m MD (2,724.0 m TVD) | Stratigraphy: Barail Coal-Shale Formation
Incident Type: MECHANICAL HOLE PACK-OFF & STUCK PIPE
Description of Event:
While drilling ahead at 2,848.0 m MD with an 8-1/2" PDC bit, drilling parameters were:
ROP = 14 m/hr, WOB = 12 tons, RPM = 110, Torque = 14 kNm, SPP = 2,820 psi, Flow = 2,350 LPM.
The driller stopped rotary and picked off bottom 2 meters to perform a drillpipe connection.
Immediately upon picking up, string motion became tight. Standpipe pressure surged to 3,650 psi
at 800 LPM, indicating severe annular obstruction. Total loss of rotation occurred.

Root Cause Analysis:
Severe sloughing and spalling of splintery carbonaceous shale and cleated coal beds between
2,835 m and 2,852 m MD. The annular velocity (120 ft/min) was insufficient to lift large coal
cuttings under laminar flow conditions during the connection pause. The coal pack accumulated
around the top stabilizer and drill collar upset.

Mitigation Procedure & Fishing Actions Taken:
1. Pumping was immediately restricted to prevent fracturing the formation at the casing shoe.
2. Spotted a 45-barrel high-density/high-viscosity sweep pill (weighted to 1.40 SG with barite
   and boosted with PAC-R polymer) across the stuck interval.
3. Allowed 45 minutes soaking time.
4. Engaged double-acting hydraulic fishing jars. Applied 85 klbs upward jarring overpull.
5. Established slow rotation at 35 RPM while working torque through string.
6. The string was successfully freed after 14.5 hours of continuous jarring and circulating.
7. Subsequent caliper log showed a 14.2-inch washout zone at 2,846 - 2,852 m MD.

Recommendations for Future Offset Wells:
- Never pause circulation for connections in Barail Coal without circulating at least 1.5 annular
  volumes at 2,400+ LPM.
- Maintain mud density at a minimum of 1.30 SG with 4% glycol to inhibit shale cleat hydration.
- In the event of initial SPP surge, pick up string immediately before the pack consolidates.
